You are labelling knee MRI studies for a research dataset. Work in /home/pavel/Programming/knee-abnormality-2026. One other labelling chat runs at the same time with its own list; never touch its files.

Instructions: work/claude_labels_oct20/_prompts/prompt.txt (read-only: never modify that folder). Follow it exactly, with the changes below; where they conflict, these changes win. Before the first study, read prompt.txt, definitions.md and schema_pilot5.json in full, and look at every image in host_figures/.

Change 1 - the panel: label only the five findings above. Each file follows schema_pilot5.json and holds "panel": "pilot5", with no structures list. Skip the prompt's sections on the other seven findings, but use every rule it gives for these five. Spend the time you save on looking: these five are focal or subtle findings.

Change 2 - every study HAS a radiology report (this replaces "There is no report: you label from the images only"):
- views.py copies it to report.txt next to the study's sheets. Read it before you look at the images, then label from the images AND the report together.
- The report was written by the radiologist who read every slice of the study; you see a subset. Treat what it states clearly as strong evidence. Use the images to decide what the report leaves vague, silent or uncertain, and to map its wording (for example "chondropathy", "bone bruise", "signal change", "degenerative", "synovial thickening") onto the host's definitions in definitions.md. Your labels are checked against expert radiologists who applied those definitions to the images.
- If the report states a finding clearly and you cannot see it on your sheets, keep it likely, unless the images clearly contradict it. If the report is silent, decide from the images alone. If report and images disagree and the images are clear, follow the images.
- In every finding's note, start with the source that decided it: "report:", "images:" or "both:".

Change 3 - zoom for focal findings: sheets 5-9 are already enlarged (0.2-0.24 mm per pixel). For every one of the five findings that is not obvious, also look at native full-resolution slices with view.py:
- medial meniscus: sagittal through the medial compartment (horns) and coronal through the joint line (body, root);
- pf_oa: axial through the patellofemoral joint (patellar and trochlear cartilage);
- contusion / fracture: the fluid-sensitive series for marrow oedema and the T1 / non-FS series for fracture lines;
- synovitis: sagittal and axial through the suprapatellar recess and Hoffa's fat pad.
Use crops (--box) to enlarge the structure. Write view PNGs only in your temp folder.

Change 4 - text reference first. Read work/claude_labels_oct20/_prompts_pilot/anchors.md. For each of its 3 anchor indexes, run views.py (below) and look at every sheet and its report.txt once, to see how clearly reported findings look on these sheets. Never save labels for anchors.

Change 5 - output: work/claude_labels_oct20/pilot_c1/ (create it): one <study_id>.json per index. No archive and no download link. Temporary files: only in work/claude_labels_oct20/_tmp_c1/. For each study (and each anchor) run, from the repo root:
  conda run -n rsna python work/claude_labels_oct20/views.py <index> --chat 1
It empties your temp folder, extracts the study's sheets into _tmp_c1/<index>/ and writes the report there as report.txt. For native slices: conda run -n rsna python work/image_labeling/view.py (usage: --help).
